Purpose, principles and limits

What this monitoring demonstration is for

Damien O’Grady Independent remote sensing and GIS consultant

This project explores how long satellite records can help mine rehabilitation teams see where vegetation patterns change, when those changes occur, and which places may deserve closer investigation.

Curragh, Queensland · Pinned snapshot of output from a working pipeline, still under development

In brief

Satellite imagery provides repeated observations over large areas. This demonstration turns those observations into maps and records of vegetation trajectory: areas that appear stable, areas with a disturbance signal, and areas with a later recovery signal. It is designed to make it easier to find patterns, follow up locations on the ground, and review how evidence was assembled.

The demonstration focuses on Curragh in Queensland. We chose Curragh because pinned monitoring outputs provide a concrete dataset to examine, and peer-reviewed research has already applied Landsat and LandTrendr to vegetation disturbance and recovery at the site. This gives the demonstration a locally relevant, research-informed starting point; its results and thresholds are specific to Curragh and should not be assumed to transfer to other mines.

The intended outcome is a practical monitoring aid for mine rehabilitation and monitoring managers and a transparent evidence interface for regulators. The longer-term aim is to support site-specific, repeatable monitoring that can direct attention and help organize evidence—not to replace field assessment or statutory completion criteria.

Beyond mine sites: the underlying approach could be adapted relatively quickly to monitor vegetation in other landscapes, subject to suitable imagery, local configuration and validation. This website shows a pinned snapshot of Curragh output from the working (but still developing) mine-rehab-monitor pipeline: the page itself does not run analyses for new areas, and Curragh's thresholds and results should not be assumed to apply elsewhere.

Closer inspection: a future extension could combine the broad-area monitoring with higher-resolution imagery to help scrutinise locations flagged for management. Such imagery is not included here and would complement rather than replace field checks.

What it does not establish: a mapped disturbance is not proof that mining caused it; a vegetation recovery label is not a rehabilitation-quality verdict; and this demonstration does not determine legal completion or compliance.

How to use the dashboard

  1. Start with the map. Choose Landsat or Sentinel-2, which are kept as separate products. Use the map modes and cohort selectors to explore class and event timing.
  2. Choose a place to investigate. Select an event point to inspect its mapped cohort and location information. Treat it as a lead for ground-truthing, not as a field observation.
  3. Inspect the fitted vegetation signal. Open Explore fitted NDVI in the dashboard menu to scrub annual maps and inspect selected event pixels and available matched controls. It displays fitted values, not the underlying observations, and makes no impact estimate.
  4. Review the evidence and its limits. The reporting and audit pages expose cohort summaries, matching-control gates, sensitivities and data-quality disclosures. The provenance page describes the pinned inputs and derived files.

BACI: comparing change with a reference

BACI stands for Before–After, Control–Impact. The basic idea is to compare change over time in an area of interest with change over the same period in suitable reference areas. If both areas change in a similar way, some of the change may reflect broader conditions such as weather or regional vegetation cycles. A difference between their before-to-after changes can be informative, provided the reference areas and study design are appropriate.

Reliable impact assessment requires more than drawing a before-and-after map. Controls need to be suitably comparable, the design needs adequate independent replication, and other possible causes must be considered. The project uses matched impact–control records and machine-checkable eligibility gates as part of this discipline. In this monitoring demonstration, the gates and summaries are available to inspect, but no causal effect estimate is presented. A gate being eligible is not itself evidence that an effect occurred.

How the vegetation stability threshold is set

NDVI is a satellite-derived indicator of vegetation condition, based on the contrast between reflected red and near-infrared light. It is useful for tracking broad patterns, but it does not directly measure plant species, overall ecological condition, or whether a rehabilitation area has met a completion criterion.

A threshold is needed to distinguish relatively small fitted changes from larger greening or browning movements. For each sensor independently, the pipeline first forms a calibration group of pixels with at least two valid observations and without a disturbance or accepted recovery event under the current event rules. For each calibration pixel it measures the absolute net change between the first and last valid fitted NDVI values. The 95th percentile of those changes sets the width of the stability band: it is a data-derived way of describing the upper end of movement in this selected reference group, rather than a number chosen by eye.

The 90th and 99th percentile alternatives are also checked to show how the resulting stable/greening/browning counts respond to a narrower or wider band. They are sensitivity checks, not three competing regulatory standards.

Important boundaries

Specialists: the Methodology & data provenance page tabulates method status, parameters, sensor comparability, 2022 confounding and the tenure disclosure.

  • The outputs are real results from a working analysis pipeline that is still under development. This web demo shows a pinned, versioned historical snapshot of them; it does not run live analysis or make Earth Engine requests, and nothing is recalculated here.
  • The two sensors remain separate. Their thresholds and products are not combined or cross-calibrated.
  • Disturbance and recovery classes describe satellite-observed trajectory patterns. They do not attribute cause or certify rehabilitation quality.
  • The 2022/T3 cohort is QA-only because attribution is under investigation; it is not evidence for mining impact.
  • Distance bands are administrative strata, not ecological boundaries. Areas are 30 m cell-area equivalents, not surveyed or statutory areas.
  • Lease-union and nested locator geometries are absent from accepted display inputs. Fitted-NDVI time series are provided in a separate standalone viewer; they are not observed values or an impact estimate.
  • Curragh thresholds are not general defaults. The frozen 15 November 2026 tenure snapshot remains in use: a 19 November comparison found one 30 m cell outside the refreshed analysis area that appears as a control in eight matching records, including four primary floor-2 records. Results were not recalculated; lease renewal status is not legally verified. This is not a current-tenure or regulatory determination.
  • This demonstration uses the ODbL option for MERIT Hydro; derived data and source notices are available via the data-licensing page. Future operational use requires its own assessment.
