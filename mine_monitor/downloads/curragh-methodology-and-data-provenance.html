Temporal & environmental confounding (2022 and the wet season)

2022 detections are not mining attribution. The required caption wherever they are shown is: “disturbance-class onset, 2022 — attribution under investigation (flood/wet-season confounding active at this site)”. Rule R-D: 2022 detections must never be labelled as mining.

The compositing window (1 April to 1 August) falls at the end of the summer-dominant wet season in a semi-arid subtropical climate (about 560 mm a year per Yang et al., 2018). A flood or an unusually wet season changes vegetation and surface moisture over wide areas at once, which can produce disturbance-like NDVI changes unrelated to land use. The project records flood and wet-season confounding as active at this site, and the pinned outputs show a widespread 2022 disturbance signal in both sensors.

Reading these together: the signal is larger outside the leases than inside, and large in both sensors, which is what a regional environmental driver would produce, but the attribution has not been resolved. The size difference between sensors (17.7% against 55.2%) is itself unexplained and may combine real differences in sensitivity, resolution and scene counts.

How the pipeline treats it

  • T3 (2022) is QA-only. The third analysis window is shown for quality assurance and is not used for primary inference. T1 (complete windows) is primary; T2 is provisional.
  • The map explorer and class tables caption 2022 onsets with the text above.
  • HAND is used only as a matching and stratification covariate. It is a flood-prone screening proxy, not a flood extent or hazard map.
  • A sensitivity that keeps Landsat 9 but drops Landsat 7 scenes in 2022 changes the Landsat count to 274,760 (+2,795). It is a diagnostic only and not adopted policy (analysis-repo audit).

What is not available (gap)

No rainfall, river-gauge, Sentinel-1 or independent flood-extent analysis exists in either repository (Sentinel-1 retrieval is planned, not implemented). There is therefore no evidence here that separates flood or wet-season effects from land-use change, and no adjustment for them is applied. The same concern applies in principle to any year, not only 2022.

Processing chain & version identifiers

  1. Grid and AOI. EPSG:7855, 30 m cells, 1,873 rows by 1,052 columns. The AOI is the 10 km buffer around the dissolved union of 14 leases (1,380.85 km²; 1,534,281 cells); the lease union is about 196.72 km² (14.25% of the AOI).
  2. Seasonal composites. Per-pixel median NDVI of QA-valid scenes from 1 April to 1 August (end exclusive) each year. A pixel needs at least five effective scenes; otherwise it is masked and a warning recorded. There is no interpolation or gap filling.
  3. Segmentation. One LandTrendr fit per sensor, run on negated NDVI so that vegetation loss appears as an increase. The two sensors share one configuration but are fitted separately.
  4. Events and classes. Disturbance and recovery rules (below) are applied to the fitted trajectories and combined into six classes.
  5. Matching and cohorts. Impact pixels are matched to controls to build cohorts and eligibility gates.
  6. Packaging. The pinned namespace is verified against recorded SHA-256 hashes and copied into the deployed site. Both packaged derived-artifact hash sets are listed in the Hash-gate & lineage checks view.

Consequences for comparing results

  • Counts, class areas and event years should be compared between sensors only qualitatively. The 2022 contrast (17.7% against 55.2% of the AOI) shows that the sensors can disagree strongly.
  • Pooled primary inference rests on Landsat T1 complete-windows; Sentinel-2 is cluster-starved with insufficient_cohort_clusters at all floors.
  • Same-date Landsat 7 and Landsat 9 scenes enter a median as separate observations, which is not de-duplicated.
Gaps (not documented or not done):
  • A Landsat–Sentinel-2 offset regression over their overlap (2016 onward) was planned but is unverified and has not been performed.
  • No diagnostic quantifies step artefacts at Landsat mission changes; the 2013 Landsat 7 → 8 year (60,082 onsets, with the Landsat 7 scan-line failure unresolved) is flagged but not corrected.
  • The Sentinel-2 baseline policy is open.
  • There is no per-scene acquisition manifest to audit which scenes enter each median.

Tenure snapshot: 15 Nov, 19 Nov discrepancy and non-recalculation

Full disclosure. All lease geometry, the analysis AOI, the distance bands and the matched-control cohorts derive from a QSpatial tenure layer frozen on 15 November 2026. A comparison against the refreshed layer on 19 November 2026 found that one 30 m cell lay outside the refreshed 10 km AOI. That cell was used as a control in 8 frozen matching records, 4 of them primary floor-2 records. On 19 November the project owner retained the 15 November snapshot. Results were not recalculated and the effect on them has not been quantified.
  • Current geometry checks validate internal consistency, not tenure currency. QSpatial re-download and revalidation remain required before public or regulatory-framing use.
  • The configuration records that ML 1878, ML 80112 and ML 80086 had renewal applications lodged with expiry in 2024–2025. Legal tenure status is unverified for those leases and for the layer as a whole.
  • Before any current-tenure or regulatory-framing use, the layer must be re-downloaded and revalidated, and the analysis recalculated or a sensitivity quantified.
  • The affected cell is a single cell in a 1,534,281-cell AOI, but its effect on the specific cohorts that used it has not been measured; this page does not claim the effect is negligible.

Known limitations

  • Rule-based classes are unvalidated, pending M4 supervised validation; no accuracy metrics are available.
  • 2022 flood/wet-season confounding is unresolved (see above); disturbance is not equivalent to mining activity.
  • A recovery label is not a rehabilitation-quality verdict and the dashboard does not determine completion or compliance.
  • Curragh thresholds are not general defaults and should not be assumed to transfer to other sites or sensors.
  • Landsat and Sentinel-2 are not radiometrically calibrated to each other and differ in resolution, density and record length.
  • Pooled primary inference rests on Landsat T1 complete-windows; Sentinel-2 is cluster-starved with insufficient_cohort_clusters at all floors.
  • The 2025 cohort contributes 47,749 of 178,562 to the cited capacity figures; interpret capacity in that context.
  • Earth Engine's live catalog has no immutable version identifier; reproducibility relies on pinned asset IDs and file hashes, not on the catalog.
  • Areas are 30 m cell-area equivalents, not surveyed or statutory areas.
  • Tenure vintage and the unrecalculated 19 November discrepancy (above).

Reproducibility & gaps

What can be reproduced from the public repository

  • The static site, the data packaging and this document are built from the dashboard repository (https://github.com/damienogrady/dash-mine-rehab-monitor); regenerate this document with python -m tools.build_methodology.
  • Every packaged derived file is pinned by SHA-256 in the namespace manifest and verified before packaging.
  • The analysis itself is in a separate repository (mine-rehab-monitor); it requires Google Earth Engine access and re-running it is not part of this web demonstration, which displays a pinned snapshot of its output and makes no Earth Engine requests.

What is needed to close the documentation gaps

The following can only come from the analysis repository or its authors and are not documented here:

  1. A machine-readable version manifest: analysis code commit at run time, Python and Earth Engine API versions, and a dependency lock.
  2. A dedicated classification version identifier tied to the rule set.
  3. A per-scene acquisition manifest (scene identifiers and dates) for each seasonal composite, and the MERIT Hydro retrieval date.
  4. The Landsat–Sentinel-2 overlap offset analysis and a diagnostic of step artefacts at Landsat mission transitions.
  5. 2022 attribution evidence (rainfall, Sentinel-1 or river-gauge analysis) and the audit diagnostics quoted above, pinned and hashed so they can be shipped with this repository.
  6. M4 supervised validation results, once the validation has been carried out.
  7. A tenure recalculation or sensitivity analysis against the refreshed 19 November layer, and a current QSpatial download.
