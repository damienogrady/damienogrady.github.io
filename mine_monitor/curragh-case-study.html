Case study · Demonstrate compliance

Reading vegetation trajectories at Curragh

Damien O’Grady Independent remote sensing and GIS consultant

Three real locations from the pinned Curragh data, chosen to show what satellite time-series analysis can reveal, what it can flag for investigation, and where it should say “this is ambiguous”.

Curragh, Queensland · Historical, unreconciled demonstration snapshot

Read this first: satellite analysis helps decide where to look and builds a traceable record. It does not replace field verification or statutory assessment, and none of the examples below is a rehabilitation-quality verdict, a compliance finding or proof of cause.

1. The site

Curragh is a coal mining area in Queensland’s Bowen Basin. The analysis area is a 10 km buffer around the dissolved union of 14 mining leases: 1,380.85 km², or 1,534,281 cells at 30 m. The lease union itself is approximately 196.72 km² (14.25% of that area). Areas in this study are 30 m cell-area equivalents, not surveyed or statutory areas, and the buffered area is not the mine footprint.

Curragh was chosen because published research has already applied Landsat and LandTrendr there (Yang et al., 2018), giving the work a relevant precedent. That paper is not a validation of this implementation. The scope here is one site, one pinned snapshot.

2. The challenge

Rehabilitation is monitored across large areas and over decades. Visiting every part of a site on a regular basis is impractical, and a single before-and-after comparison can miss when a change happened, whether it persisted, or whether it was a one-off. Managers and regulators need a repeatable way to see where vegetation has changed, when, and which locations deserve inspection, with a record of how each conclusion was reached.

3. The approach

  • Landsat and Sentinel-2. Landsat provides a record from 1988; Sentinel-2 provides a record from 2016. The two products are analysed separately and are not cross-calibrated. The examples below use Landsat because its record is long enough to show recovery.
  • GIS. Everything is held on a shared 30 m grid (EPSG:7855, MGA2020 Zone 55), so pixels, lease context, distance strata and flood-proneness strata can be combined and traced.
  • Trajectory analysis. A LandTrendr-style temporal fit of annual NDVI identifies disturbance onset and, where present, recovery. A rule-based adaptation then labels each mapped disturbance, for example DNR (disturbed, no accepted recovery), DHR and DLR (disturbance with higher-rate or lower-rate recovery).

The figures below summarise the fitted NDVI of one mapped event component: the line is the median across its cells and the shaded band is the inter-quartile range. They are generated from the pinned data by tools/case_study_figures.py; no new analysis was run.

4. Findings: three examples

Each example is a mapped Landsat event component (a group of adjacent cells sharing an onset year). They were chosen to illustrate three different situations, not as typical or representative results.

A. Sustained vegetation improvement

Derived measurement
Median fitted NDVI falls from 0.475 (1990) to 0.162 (1993), then rises almost every year to 0.440 (2026).
Classification
Onset 1990, accepted recovery 1993; 168 of 170 cells are DLR (lower-rate recovery) and 2 are DNR. This is a T1 cohort and the window is not gapped.
Interpretation
A sustained, multi-decade increase in vegetation greenness after a disturbance. It is consistent with vegetation re-establishing, but NDVI cannot say whether the vegetation is the intended species, structure or land use.

The fitted NDVI in 2026 is still below its 1990 level, and “lower-rate recovery” is a trajectory-rate label, not a quality verdict.

B. An anomalous trajectory: possible disturbance

Derived measurement
Median fitted NDVI is 0.405 in 2016, 0.159 in 2017 and about 0.13 in every year from 2018 to 2026.
Classification
Mapped onset 2016; 1,474 of 1,521 cells are DNR (disturbed, no accepted recovery), 25 DHR and 22 DLR. T1 cohort, window not gapped.
Interpretation
A sharp, persistent loss of vegetation greenness that has not reversed. It is a potential disturbance that merits a closer look, for example a site visit or higher-resolution imagery.

The analysis does not say why the drop occurred. Clearing, earthworks, fire, a change of land use or a data artefact would all need to be ruled in or out from other evidence, and mining is not assumed to be the cause.

C. Where conditions complicate interpretation

Derived measurement
Median fitted NDVI is about 0.31 for 2019–2021, jumps to 0.589 in 2022, and returns to 0.297 in 2023.
Classification
Mapped as a 2022 disturbance onset; 17,070 of 17,183 cells are DNR. The 2022 cohort is held as T3 (QA-only).
Interpretation
This is not read as a disturbance. The dominant feature is a one-year rise across a very large area, followed by a return to the previous level. That is the signature of a transient condition, not of vegetation loss.

The data pipeline flags the 2022 cohort because flood and wet-season confounding is active at this site: attribution is under investigation (methodology: confounding). A transient, widespread 2022 signal fits that concern, but this analysis has not established the cause. The method’s value here is that it makes the ambiguity visible and withholds the cohort from impact evidence, instead of reporting 17,183 cells as disturbed.

5. Limitations

  • Flooding and flood-prone ground. Flood-prone areas are a HAND screening proxy only, not a statutory flood extent, flood hazard map or 0.5% AEP floodplain.
  • Seasonal and wet-season effects. Annual NDVI can respond to rainfall timing, as example C shows. The two sensors’ thresholds are not cross-calibrated.
  • Classification uncertainty. Classes are a rule-based adaptation, unvalidated, pending supervised (M4) validation. Trajectory-rate label fragility is 24.26% for Landsat and 7.19% for Sentinel-2. Event components can mix classes (examples A and B each contain a minority of cells in other classes).
  • Fitted, not observed. The figures use fitted NDVI summarised over cells; they conceal within-component variation beyond the inter-quartile band.
  • Historical, unreconciled snapshot. The demonstration keeps its 15 November 2026 tenure snapshot. A 19 November comparison found one 30 m cell outside the refreshed analysis area, and results have not been recalculated. It is not a current-tenure or regulatory determination.
  • Local, not general. Curragh thresholds are not general defaults, and these results should not be assumed to transfer to other sites.

See the monitoring approach report for the full list of boundaries, and Methodology & data provenance for method status, sensor comparability and reproducibility gaps.

6. Practical value for mine operators and consultants

  • Targeting. Rank locations for inspection using trajectories, so field time goes where the signal is strongest or most uncertain (examples A to C each lead to a different action).
  • Timing. Show when a change started and whether it persisted, a question a single image pair cannot answer.
  • Honest uncertainty. Separate clear patterns from ambiguous ones before they reach a report.
  • Traceable records. Every output in this demonstration is hash-pinned and linked to its provenance, so an evidence trail can be reviewed and repeated.
  • Complement to field work. The output is a lead for ground-truthing and a context layer for assessments, never a substitute for field verification or statutory criteria.
