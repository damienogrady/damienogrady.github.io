The three pillars

Vegetation and ecosystem monitoring

Problem: Governments, landowners and regulators need to know what condition vegetation and ecosystems are in, how that’s changing, and whether interventions are working. And it doesn’t stop at natural ecosystems: operators of mine sites — and of any other site where regulators require monitoring and rehabilitation of disturbed ground — must produce objective evidence that disturbance is being managed and recovery is progressing.

Deliver: Satellite time-series monitoring of vegetation extent, condition and change — habitat and vegetation mapping, fire scar and regrowth tracking, land-clearing and habitat-loss detection, fine-scale classification down to individual trees using machine-learning image segmentation. For disturbed-ground sites — mines, quarries, construction and demolition sites, corridors and other regulated operations — change detection over the full satellite record, customised to your site and monitored over time, delivered in a form your team and your regulators can use. Pipelines your team can operate, or a standing monitoring service.

Holds up: Time-series separates real trends from seasonal noise — a single image can mislead; a decade of them rarely does. For rehabilitation sites, that means recovery trajectories measured from the satellite record rather than asserted from inspections — matched-control comparisons that show whether an intervention actually worked, in the language regulators use. Methods documented and reproducible, which matters when a clearing-detection finding or a rehabilitation report becomes evidence in a compliance or planning dispute.

See the mine rehabilitation monitor for a working demonstration — Curragh only · pinned snapshot · not a live service. The system behind it is built and reusable, and can be applied to any client’s site.

Water extent mapping and monitoring

Problem: You need to know where the water is, where it was, and where it’s going — floods as they happen, lakes and wetlands shrinking or growing, soil moisture across a catchment. Decisions hang on those numbers, and they have to hold up.

Deliver: Water extent and change mapping from multispectral and radar (SAR) satellite data, from a single wetland to a whole catchment — flood extent maps for emergency response, time-series of lake and wetland extent, catchment-scale surface-water and soil-moisture monitoring. Built as automated Python pipelines, not one-off runs: refresh with each satellite pass, not each invoice.

Holds up: Radar sees through cloud and darkness — flood mapping doesn’t wait for weather. Every map is traceable, defensible and reproducible: source imagery, processing chain and parameters documented, so the analysis survives scrutiny and can be rerun or audited.

Related demonstrations: Flood Extent Explorer and Aral Sea Waterline Change.
